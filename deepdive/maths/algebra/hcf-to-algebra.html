Deep Dive: Highest Common Factor

From factors of numbers all the way to factorising algebraic expressions

Stage 1

What is a Highest Common Factor?

The factors of a number are all the whole numbers that divide into it exactly.

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6  →  Highest Common Factor (HCF) = 6
Stage 2

Finding HCF using Prime Factorisation

Listing factors gets slow for bigger numbers. Instead, break each number into prime factors, then multiply the primes they share.

36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
Shared primes: 2 × 2 × 3 = HCF = 12
Stage 3

HCF of Algebraic Terms

The same idea applies to terms with letters. Find the HCF of the numbers, then take the lowest power of any letter common to all terms.

HCF of 8x²y and 12xy³:
Numbers: HCF(8,12) = 4
x: lowest power is x¹   y: lowest power is y¹
HCF = 4xy
Stage 4

Using HCF to Factorise Expressions

Factorising means writing an expression as the HCF multiplied by what's left over in brackets.

Factorise 6x² + 9x:
HCF of 6x² and 9x is 3x
6x² ÷ 3x = 2x    9x ÷ 3x = 3
6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)
